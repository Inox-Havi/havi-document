# Technical Summary: Pitting Corrosion in Stainless Steel

## Core Specifications & Overview
Pitting corrosion is a localized electrochemical degradation mechanism driven by chloride ions (Cl⁻) that rupture the passive chromium oxide (Cr₂O₃) film on stainless steel. This process triggers an autocatalytic micro-galvanic cell, causing rapid through-wall penetration while leaving surrounding surfaces intact.

## Material Parameters & Standards
- **Passive Film:** Formed by a minimum of 10.5% – 18% Chromium content.
- **PREN Formula:** $\text{PREN} = \%\text{Cr} + 3.3 \times (\%\text{Mo} + 0.5 \times \%\text{W}) + 16 \times \%\text{N}$
- **Critical Alloy:** Addition of 2.0% - 3.0% Molybdenum in SUS 316/316L elevates PREN above 25.
- **Surface Treatment Standards:** ASTM A380 for pickling and passivation post-welding.

## Practical Applications & Prevention
- Marine environments and coastal architecture.
- Chemical storage tanks and industrial piping systems.
- Food processing equipment exposed to saline or chloride-based sanitizers.
- Material selection optimization via HaVi Việt Nam.
